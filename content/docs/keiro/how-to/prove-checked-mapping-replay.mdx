---
title: "Prove checked mapping replay before adoption"
description: "Compare baseline and candidate captures over retained history, choose rollout constraints, and keep retirement decisions separate."
---

Use this when adopting a checked mapping or changing ID admission in an existing service. You need
both service revisions, their dependency plans, and a retained-history corpus with fixed stream
high-water marks. [Current-value conformance](/docs/keiro/how-to/prove-aggregate-behavior-is-complete)
is a prerequisite; it does not replace historical replay.

## Goal

Produce matching baseline/candidate semantic captures with no compatibility failures, then choose
a rollout that preserves old readers for every retained history still permitted by the service.

## Derive the inventory first

Use `Keiro.Test.ReplayCompatibility` from `keiro-test-support` 0.18. Independently combine old and
new persisted surfaces, ordinary compatibility findings, aggregate replay impacts, mapped
consequences, checked process reactions and application-owned obligations. Include all seven
inventory contributions, with a reason for each that does not apply. Never derive required cases
only from the reports that happen to have been produced.

Cover historical reads, semantic equivalence, old-reader/new-writer compatibility, snapshot
invalidation/full replay, process-manager replay and workflow replay where applicable. For workflows,
include decoded results, continuation traces, effects and durable identities; a successful aggregate
replay cannot waive those obligations.

## Capture each build separately

Build each revision independently so old and new domain types need not coexist in one executable.
Bind both reports to the exact source revisions, language/runtime profiles and dependency-plan
hashes. Use the same corpus, high-water marks, observation contract, clock, randomness, external
response schedule and failure schedule. Capture state, continuations, identities and fresh
allocations through real decoders and runtime replay.

For noncanonical dates, duplicate/permuted sets or uppercase hex, also call `checkNormalizationLaw`
with raw historical bytes and their canonical replacement inside the same prefix/suffix history.
The canonical writer must preserve the domain and replay observations.

## Compare and choose the rollout

Call `validateCompatibility inventory baselineReport candidateReport` and retain its full failure
list. `releaseReady` must return `True`. Missing, duplicated, failed, unverified or empty required
observations block adoption; do not turn them into a success by deleting inventory cases.

Choose the rollout from the actual changed boundary:

- When widening ID admission, deploy readers that accept v5 before allowing v5 writers. When
  narrowing it, retain the wider reader until audited retained history proves that narrowing safe.
- A calendar-day reader that cannot accept the candidate's extended years requires reader-first,
  producer-last rollout. A shared `Day` type alone does not prove old-reader compatibility.
- Preserve public-contract, queue and process/workflow codec obligations independently of the
  private-event mapping. Version codecs or drain only where the retained-history policy permits it.
- For workflow result changes, keep versioned decoding or a recorded patch branch with the old
  reader and key. A renamed step can execute a new effect; use a new key only for an intentional
  new action on the new branch.

## Verify it worked

Archive the inventory, both build identities, corpus/high-water marks and both reports. Confirm
zero failures, nonempty observations for every required case, and a rollout constraint for each
old-reader hazard. Regenerate and compile the runtime and conformance packages as a separate check.
The checked-mapping release rehearsal is evidence for its committed fixtures, not blanket
permission to remove predecessor implementations from another application.
